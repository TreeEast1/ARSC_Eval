# ARSC claims and terminology

This document fixes what ARSC *is* in the paper, what each axis name is
allowed to mean, and which claims the frozen BDD-OIA experiments do and do not
support. It is the reference the other paper assets defer to. Where a number
appears here it is quoted from a frozen artifact and the artifact path is
given; the tables and figures under `outputs/paper/` are the computed source of
truth and are regenerated by script.

## 1. What ARSC is

ARSC is **an auditable multi-axis evaluation protocol for decision models**.
It is not a safety score, not a benchmark ranking, and not a certification
procedure.

Its central claim is:

> When two decision models have comparable Action Performance, action
> Macro-F1 alone hides differences that matter. Rationale-label Performance,
> Selective Risk & Calibration, and Prediction Stability carry non-redundant
> information and change the judgement. ARSC does not fuse these into a single
> safety score; it requires that they be reported separately.

The operational consequence is the punchline of the main result:

> **Action Performance produces a ranking. ARSC produces a profile.**

There is deliberately **no aggregate ARSC score**. The axes are not
commensurable, and Round 10 demonstrates empirically that they do not respond
to the same manipulation, so collapsing them would discard exactly the
information the protocol exists to expose.

## 2. The four axes, as operationalised in this repository

The axis letters are labels for specific measurements on BDD-OIA. Each axis is
paired with an explicit statement of what it does **not** measure, because in
every case there is a stronger reading available that this evidence does not
support.

### A - Action Performance

* **Operationalisation:** 4-action Macro-F1 at threshold 0.5.
* **Reads as:** how well the model recovers the annotated action set.
* **Does NOT mean:** a safety guarantee, or task competence beyond this label
  set.

### R - Rationale-label Performance

* **Operationalisation:** 21-class rationale Macro-F1 and Micro-F1 at
  threshold 0.5.
* **Reads as:** the model's ability to recover the annotated rationale
  **labels**.
* **Does NOT mean:** reasoning faithfulness. R says nothing about whether the
  model's internal computation used the reason it names, and a high or low R
  does not show that the model's explanations are correct or incorrect. The
  correct phrase for a low R is **"rationale-label recovery coverage is
  incomplete and highly class-dependent"** - never "the model does not
  reason" or "the model's explanation is wrong".

### S - Selective Risk & Calibration

* **Operationalisation:** AURC, UAR@90 and ECE, computed on temperature-scaled
  probabilities against an exact-set error (any of the four thresholded action
  bits wrong), with the frozen confidence definition `conf = max_i p_i`.
* **Reads as:** a selective-prediction operating characteristic - how well the
  model's confidence ranking supports abstention.
* **Does NOT mean:** "Safety". AURC, UAR@90 and ECE must not be collectively
  renamed safety. They are three separate quantities that, as the audit in
  `outputs/paper/tables/s_confidence_audit.md` shows, do not agree with each
  other under any of the three confidence constructions tested.

### C - Prediction Stability under semantics-preserving perturbations

Written `C1` in the code and result files.

* **Operationalisation:** clean vs perturbed thresholded action-set flip rate
  at 0.5, under brightness, Gaussian blur and deterministic Gaussian noise.
* **Reads as:** how often the model's discrete action set changes when the
  input is perturbed in a way that does not change the correct answer.
* **Does NOT mean:** real-road robustness, or evidence faithfulness. The
  perturbations are synthetic pixel operations on BDD-OIA frames, not a model
  of real driving conditions, and a low flip rate does not show the model
  relies on correct evidence.

### CEG - not an axis

The Causal Evidence Gap was intended as an evidence-sensitivity extension. It
is **not a validated primary axis** and is not reported as one. It appears in
the paper only as a failed extension and a measurement boundary; see
`docs/paper/LIMITATIONS.md` section 3 and section 5 below.

## 3. The two research questions

### RQ1 - Does multi-axis evaluation change the judgement reached from Action Performance alone?

Evidence: Round 5, the five-paired-seed BDD-OIA study (seeds 43-47).

Action Performance is practically equivalent under the pre-registered
+/-0.03 margin, which is what makes the case diagnostically useful: A cannot
arbitrate between the two models, so any real difference has to be looked for
elsewhere. The other three axes then give a *profile* rather than a winner -
R exposes coverage holes, S resolves on only one of its three metrics, and C
gives the best-supported non-A difference but is not unanimous across seeds.

Assets: `outputs/paper/tables/arsc_profile.md` (Profile Table) and
`outputs/paper/tables/decision_change.md` (Decision Change Table).

### RQ2 - Do the four axes provide non-redundant information?

Evidence: Round 10, the synthetic pixel perturbation dose-response experiment
(4557 test images, 3904 source clips, three families, clean plus four severity
levels, real re-inference).

The pre-registered verdict is `ROUND10_PARTIAL_OR_FAIL`: 3 of 12
family-by-axis gates passed. **That verdict and its thresholds are unchanged.**
What the paper adds is the observation that **all three passing gates are C1
gates**, while aggregate A and R stay nearly flat and S does not even respond
with a consistent sign across families.

The correct reading is therefore *not* "only 3/12, so the four axes failed".
It is:

> Only C responds to this perturbation manipulation, and that is precisely
> discriminant / non-redundancy evidence: input perturbation can produce a
> large number of single-sample prediction-set changes while aggregate Action
> Macro-F1 is essentially unchanged. C and A are not the same measurement, and
> R and S do not move in lockstep with either.

This does **not** license the claim that every perturbation should move every
axis, and it does **not** constitute construct validation of the axes.

Assets: `outputs/paper/figures/round10_axis_separation.png` and
`outputs/paper/tables/round10_axis_separation.md`.

## 4. Seed heterogeneity is a result, not noise

Across the five frozen seeds, **no headline model comparison in the profile is
unanimous**. Seed 43 reverses the C1 stability advantage (in Round 5 and again
in Round 12), seed 45 reverses the AURC advantage, seed 46 reverses ECE, and
the Action Macro-F1 advantage itself holds on only 3 of 5 seeds.

These reversals are reported verbatim. No seed is dropped, reweighted, or
explained away, and no cause is attributed to them. The reporting rule is:

> The ARSC profile shows clear training-seed heterogeneity, so means must
> always be reported together with the seed-level distribution.

Asset: `outputs/paper/figures/seed_heterogeneity.png` and
`outputs/paper/tables/seed_heterogeneity.md`.

## 6. Permitted claims

1. Joint and Action-Only have comparable Action Performance within the
   pre-defined practical-equivalence band.
2. Action F1 alone misses differences in rationale coverage, selective-risk
   behaviour, and perturbation stability.
3. Round 10 shows prediction-set stability can degrade markedly while
   aggregate Macro-F1 is nearly unchanged, so these dimensions are not
   redundant.
4. Joint shows lower mean perturbation-induced action flip in this BDD-OIA
   case study, with seed heterogeneity.
5. Rationale performance exposes clear class coverage holes.
6. The selective-risk conclusion depends on metric and confidence
   operationalisation and must be reported per item.
7. ARSC's value is an auditable evaluation profile, not a single score.

## 7. Forbidden claims

The following must not appear, in any phrasing:

* Joint is comprehensively safer.
* Rationale supervision improves all dimensions.
* Rationale F1 proves faithful reasoning.
* C1 proves the model relies on correct evidence.
* The BDD-OIA experiments prove nuclear safety.
* CEG has been validated.
* All four axes passed construct validation.
* AURC / UAR@90 / ECE collectively constitute "Safety".
* The driving experiments constitute nuclear safety validation.

## 8. Reproducibility rule

Every table and figure under `outputs/paper/` is generated by a script in
`scripts/` that reads only frozen artifacts under `outputs/validity/`. No
result number is transcribed by hand into code as a source of computation. The
frozen artifacts themselves are never modified.

Regenerate everything with:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/build_all_paper_assets.py
```

The S confidence audit re-derives the frozen `S0` values and asserts they match
the published numbers in `outputs/validity/rq1_multiseed_summary.json` to
within 1e-9 before any alternative construction is compared against them.
